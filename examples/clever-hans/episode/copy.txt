The Horse That Could Count (Until Nobody Knew the Answer)

Clever Hans seemed to do arithmetic in early-1900s Germany, tapping answers with his hoof. A 13-member commission found no cheating. Then psychologist Oskar Pfungst showed Hans got the right answer only when the questioner knew it and Hans could see them: he was reading tiny involuntary cues. That's why careful experiments are double-blind.

We test the SPIKE router the same way: 130 requests written blind and never used for tuning. It got 116 right (0.89). Link in @oddlyprovenai bio.
